# Diagnostic Catalog: Common IELTS Writing Mistakes & Traps

This diagnostic reference catalogs the most frequent errors that keep non-native candidates trapped at Band 6 or 6.5, providing the precise Band 8 remedies.

---

## 1. Grammatical & Syntactical Traps

### Mistake 1: The Comma Splice (Two Clauses Joined Only by a Comma)
- **The Error**: Joining two complete independent clauses with nothing but a comma.
  - *Weak*: "The government built new highways, traffic congestion increased anyway."
- **Why It Penalizes GRA**: An examiner sees this as a lack of sentence boundary control.
- **The Band 8 Fix**:
  - *Fix A (Concession)*: "Although the government constructed new highways, traffic congestion increased nonetheless."
  - *Fix B (Semicolon + Conjunctive Adverb)*: "The government constructed new highways; however, traffic congestion continued to escalate."
  - *Fix C (Participle clause)*: "The government constructed new highways, yet failed to curb escalating traffic congestion."

---

### Mistake 2: Missing or Superfluous Articles (*A*, *An*, *The*)
- **The Error**: Dropping *the* before specific nouns or inserting *the* before generalized uncountables.
  - *Weak*: "Government should provide education to poor."
  - *Weak*: "The pollution is bad for the human health."
- **Why It Happens**: Hindi and other Indian regional languages lack an explicit article system.
- **The Band 8 Fix**:
  - *Rule 1*: Plural or uncountable nouns used in a general sense take **zero article**: *"Pollution is detrimental to human health."*
  - *Rule 2*: Specific societal institutions or defined groups take *the*: *"The government should provide subsidized education to the underprivileged."*

---

### Mistake 3: Subject-Verb Agreement with Collective or Quantified Nouns
- **The Error**: *"The number of cars are increasing"* or *"Every students need to learn"*.
- **The Band 8 Rule**:
  - `The number of + [Plural Noun]` = **Singular Verb** (*"The number of electric vehicles **is** increasing"*).
  - `A number of + [Plural Noun]` = **Plural Verb** (*"A number of researchers **have** demonstrated..."*).
  - `Every / Each + [Singular Noun]` = **Singular Verb** (*"Every student **needs** individual mentorship"*).

## 2. Lexical & Stylistic Pitfalls

### Mistake 4: Forced "Sophisticated" Vocabulary (The Thesaurus Trap)
- **The Error**: Shoving archaic, formal, or high-flown words into normal sentences.
  - *Weak*: "This deleterious conundrum will bring a plethora of vicissitudes to humanity."
- **Examiner's Reaction**: Band 5/6 for Lexical Resource due to unnatural, comical usage.
- **The Band 8 Fix**: Use natural, precise, topic-appropriate collocations:
  - *Improved*: "This multifaceted crisis poses profound socioeconomic challenges for urban populations."

---

### Mistake 5: Mechanical Over-Linking (The "Furthermore / Moreover" Addiction)
- **The Error**: Starting every single sentence with an adverbial connector:
  - *Weak*: "Firstly, public parks improve health. Furthermore, they reduce stress. In addition, people can meet friends. Moreover, children can play."
- **Examiner's Reaction**: Band 6 for Coherence & Cohesion (*"Uses mechanical linking devices excessively"*).
- **The Band 8 Fix**: Use **referential cohesion** (demonstratives, relative clauses, lexical bridges):
  - *Band 8*: "Public parks contribute substantially to community well-being. These recreational spaces not only encourage physical exercise but also provide communal hubs where urban residents can decompress and cultivate neighborhood bonds."

---

## 3. Task Response & Argumentation Traps

### Mistake 6: The Circular Argument (Restating the Claim as the Reason)
- **The Error**: Repeating the prompt statement using different words instead of explaining the causal mechanism.
  - *Weak*: "Free university education is good because students can study at university without paying any money."
- **Examiner's Reaction**: Underdeveloped main ideas -> Capped at Band 6 for Task Response.
- **The Band 8 Fix (Apply C-R-E-I)**:
  - *(Claim)* Abolishing tertiary tuition fees fosters long-term socioeconomic mobility.
  - *(Reason)* Bright individuals from lower-income backgrounds are frequently barred by prohibitive debt burdens.
  - *(Explanation)* When fee barriers are dismantled, admissions depend solely on academic merit, allowing marginalized youths to enter high-value professions like medicine and engineering.
  - *(Impact)* In the long run, this democratizes the national talent pool and reduces intergenerational poverty cycles.

---

### Mistake 7: The Drifting Thesis (Wavering Position)
- **The Error**: Claiming to "Strongly Agree" in the Introduction, writing body paragraphs that praise the opposite view without refuting it, and then concluding with "It depends".
- **The Band 8 Fix**:
  - Formulate your exact position during your 3-minute brainstorm.
  - State your stance unequivocally in the introduction.
  - Align both body paragraphs with your thesis (or explicitly frame opposing arguments as subordinate concessions).
  - Reaffirm your stance decisively in the conclusion.
